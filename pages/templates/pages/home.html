How much fresh food should you order for tomorrow?

Binless forecasts each product's sales as a range, turns that range into an order that weighs the cost of waste against the cost of an empty shelf, and explains each line in plain English.

See tomorrow's order

What it found

  • In a four-week test on 50 stores, ordering from Binless's forecast instead of reordering last week's sales cut waste by about 30% and served more of the demand.
  • On the dataset's held-out week across all 898 stores, its forecast error matched the dataset authors' published models: 31.6%, against 31.6% to 32.0%.

On 44% of store-product days a product sold out at some point, so sales records understate what customers wanted. That's why the order page lets you choose how much an empty shelf matters.

Results come from FreshRetailNet-50K, a public dataset of fresh-food sales (CC BY 4.0). The method, code and the command behind every number are on GitHub.
