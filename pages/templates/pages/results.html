Results

These figures come from a public dataset of real fresh-food sales, not from the simulated shop on the order page.

Ordering from the forecast wastes less

Over the last four weeks of a 50-store sample, ordering Binless's median forecast instead of last week's sales cut waste from 20.6% to 14.6% of what was ordered and served more of the demand. Ordering higher up the forecast range, at the 80th or 90th percentile, runs short on far fewer days at the cost of more waste. The order page's slider is that trade-off.

It matches published models on a held-out week

On the dataset's evaluation week across all 898 stores, scored the way the dataset's authors score their models, Binless's error is in line with their models trained on recorded sales. Their best model, trained on demand recovered by a learned model, is 2.55 points better. I didn't rerun their code: their figures come from their paper.
